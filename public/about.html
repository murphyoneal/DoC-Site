What this is

Most property data tells you what it found.
Almost none tells you what it missed.

A blank field can mean two opposite things: we looked and there was nothing, or we never held the data at all. Read the wrong way, one of those becomes a clearance nobody gave.

So every finding carries its state, its source and its date. A gap says it's a gap, and names the office that can answer it. That's a checklist item routed correctly — not a failure.

The records come from the agencies that keep them: FEMA, FDEP, USFWS, the National Park Service, the Department of Revenue, and each county's property appraiser and clerk.

Sixty-seven counties, no two structured alike. Ten formats for a parcel number. One county publishing its identifier wrapped in an HTML tag. Assembling that is the work.

Where a figure is a floor rather than a total, the report says so. Wetland acreage excludes open water and deepwater — the difference between 26.6% of Florida and the 60% you get by counting a lake as a wetland.

Every physical change should leave a trace in more than one register.

Of 16,359 recorded marine improvements in one county, most have a building permit within a year of the recorded build year. Thousands do not — and most of those sit on parcels that do have permits. That isn't a value. It's a question worth asking, with the evidence attached.

Who it's for

Two questions, the same record.

Before you list it

You're already the person who spots what the buyer missed. This is what you currently can't see — the groundwater restriction, the seawall, the improvement with no permit behind it.

An agent who finds it first controls it. One who learns it from the buyer's inspector has lost the deal.

Before you buy it

Same record, different question. Not how do I manage this, but should I proceed — and what do I ask for.

You get the findings, the authority behind each one, and a list of what to request from whom.

Privacy

We tell agents about the property.
Never about you.

Roughly four in five buyers start looking without an agent, on purpose. Nothing here changes that.

Reports are keyed to the parcel, not to a person. There is no people-search. An agent sees that a property drew interest — never who.

We don't publish a profile for anyone who hasn't claimed one. A licence is verified against the state register before a name appears beside a report.

Counts, not scores. What's in the record is what we'll say.

Department of Property holds land and property records. Department of Construction holds contractor and permit records. Both are technology companies. Neither is a licensing authority, a government agency, or affiliated with any government body.

Parcel outlines are approximate boundaries drawn from county tax records. They are not surveys. Findings are a starting point for due diligence, not a substitute for a title search, a survey, a jurisdictional determination or an inspection.
