# Florida 67‑County Tax Deed / Lands Available Register Work‑List

## TL;DR
- **Almost no Florida county publishes a Lands Available register a script can fetch with a plain GET.** Of 67 counties, only a small set return the register as directly-fetchable static HTML/PDF with real data (verified: **Putnam** — a rich parcel-level table; **Indian River** — a clean static table; **Sumter** — a downloadable PDF; **Hillsborough** — a downloadable weekly spreadsheet; plus small static lists at Holmes, Taylor, Union). Several more (Columbia, Gulf, Hamilton, Wakulla, Nassau) return fetchable static pages that are currently empty. The high-parcel metros overwhelmingly route Lands Available through a JavaScript "TaxSmart/RealTDM" search portal, the RealAuction/RealTaxDeed third-party platform, or an email/records request — the same click-through friction Volusia has, or worse.
- **The three register types publish unevenly.** The upcoming-sale calendar is nearly always on a third-party auction platform — `{county}.realtaxdeed.com` (RealAuction) is the dominant pattern, with a minority on RealForeclose, Bid4Assets, Grant Street ClerkAuction, or LienHub, and a handful still in-person. Lands Available and Surplus Funds are Clerk-maintained but frequently sit behind a disclaimer, a status-filter search form, or a paid quote request.
- **First pulls should weigh parcel count against access ease.** Putnam and Indian River are the only clean static lists with data today; Hillsborough and Sumter are downloadable; the largest metros (Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Orange) carry the highest audit value per parcel but must be scripted as form-POST / click-through jobs.

## Key Findings

1. **The RealAuction "RealTaxDeed" subdomain pattern is the backbone of upcoming-sale publishing.** Confirmed live in research: `pasco.realtaxdeed.com`, `duval.realtaxdeed.com`, `marion.realtaxdeed.com`, `baker.realtaxdeed.com`, `washington.realtaxdeed.com`, `citrus.realtaxdeed.com`, `hernando.realtaxdeed.com`, `lake.realtaxdeed.com`, `seminole.realtaxdeed.com`, `nassau.realtaxdeed.com`, and `bay.realtaxdeed.com`. This is a reliable inference pattern, but each subdomain must be verified before it is trusted in a seed file.

2. **Not everyone uses RealAuction.** Manatee runs sales on `manatee.realforeclose.com` (also a RealAuction product, foreclosure-branded); Okaloosa uses **Bid4Assets**; St. Lucie uses **Grant Street ClerkAuction®**; Flagler's tax deed *application* pipeline and Pinellas's *certificate* sales run through **LienHub**; Collier, Franklin, Liberty, Dixie and several rural counties run **in-person** auctions. GovEase is widely used for tax sales in other states, but I did not confirm a Florida tax-deed county currently on GovEase.

3. **Lands Available formats fall into five buckets:** (a) directly-fetchable static HTML/PDF/spreadsheet with data — Putnam, Indian River, Sumter (PDF), Hillsborough (weekly spreadsheet), Holmes, Taylor, Union; (b) fetchable static statements that are currently empty — Columbia, Gulf, Hamilton, Wakulla, Nassau; (c) JavaScript search portals (Pioneer TaxSmart / Landmark / RealTDM / Tribute) — Brevard, Bay, Clay, Charlotte, Hernando, Citrus, St. Johns, St. Lucie, Orange, Polk, Osceola, Sarasota, Miami-Dade; (d) third-party auction platform only — most RealTaxDeed counties; (e) request-only (email/phone the Tax Deed dept, sometimes a fee) — Escambia, Broward, Okaloosa, Bradford, Suwannee, Madison, Jefferson, Liberty, Franklin, Gilchrist, Walton, Clay, Levy (Tax Collector holds the list).

4. **Volusia's friction is the median, not an outlier.** Volusia's click-through-disclaimer PDF report (`app02.clerk.org/cm_rpt/?id=lat`) is roughly the typical experience. Broward is worse: it is a **County Records, Taxes & Treasury** function (not the Clerk), and requires a quote request — per Broward, *"Requests for quotes are accepted Monday through Friday. Up to three quotes per individual per day can be requested. Quotes are typically available within 3-5 business days."* Escambia charges to produce the number — per its Clerk, *"To request a Lands Available purchase quote please email taxdeeds@escambiaclerk.com. There is a $7.00 fee to prepare the purchase statement."* Indian River and Putnam are materially better than all of these (plain static tables).

5. **Field coverage is consistent where lists exist.** The most common published fields are certificate number, case number, parcel ID, original opening bid, original sale date, and escheatment date — Indian River publishes exactly these. Owner name and legal description appear on the portal-based systems (Putnam, Brevard, Bay). Assessed value is rarely published directly. Opening bids are stale almost everywhere — Indian River, Taylor, Brevard, and Marion all warn the listed amount is no longer the opening bid and that only the Clerk/Tax Collector can quote the current price.

6. **Beware lookalike/commercial domains.** taxsaleresources.com, taxnetusa.com, propertychecker.com, floridapropertyrecords.us, visionarysurplusrecovery.com, countyoffice.org, and registryofdeeds.org are NOT official. Use only Clerk `.com/.org/.gov` domains verified against the county's own branding. RealAuction, LienHub, Bid4Assets and Grant Street are legitimate official vendors, but they are third-party, not the Clerk's own site.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Prove the pipeline on the two clean static lists (this week).** Pull **Putnam** first: it is the only county returning a rich, parcel-level table (cert#, owner, legal, parcel, auction date, available date, estimated purchase price, ~35+ rows) on a plain GET, so it is the ideal template and the strongest immediate audit corpus. Then **Indian River**, which publishes exactly the audit fields you already trust from Volusia (cert#, parcel, opening bid, sale date, escheatment date). Success benchmark: both tables parse to structured rows and every parcel ID resolves against your Property Appraiser data.

**Stage 2 — Add the downloadable-file counties (next).** **Hillsborough** (weekly Tax Deed spreadsheet — very large parcel base, best value-to-effort ratio of any big county) and **Sumter** (LAFT PDF, fast-growing Villages market). Build a PDF/spreadsheet parser here; note Hillsborough's case-number format on the spreadsheet is `YYYY-CaseNumber` (e.g., `2016-142`, not `16-LW-000142`) — normalize before joins. Round out the cheap tier with **Holmes**, **Taylor**, and **Union**, and set the five "currently empty" static pages (Columbia, Gulf, Hamilton, Wakulla, Nassau) on a recurring poll.

**Stage 3 — Script the high-value metros as form-POST / click-through jobs.** In descending audit value: **Miami-Dade** (largest parcel base; run the "public-lands avail for Tax" report + bidder registration), **Orange** (status-filter search), **Palm Beach** (click-through + Lands Available tab), **Brevard** and **Marion** (single click-through). These carry the most ground-truth test cases, so the engineering cost of a headless-browser/form-POST fetcher is justified. Benchmark to advance: a headless fetch reliably clears the disclaimer/search and returns ≥1 full parcel row per county.

**Stage 4 — Handle request-only and platform-only counties manually or via vendor.** For RealTaxDeed/RealForeclose/Bid4Assets/ClerkAuction counties, evaluate whether the vendor exposes a per-county JSON/XML endpoint before building scrapers. For request-only rural counties (Bradford, Suwannee, Madison, Jefferson, Liberty, Franklin, Gilchrist), a quarterly email to the Tax Deed department is cheaper than automation given their tiny registers. Escambia's $7-per-quote and Broward's 3-quotes-per-day limits make bulk pulls impractical there — treat as targeted, not comprehensive.

**Standing rule for every county:** never treat a scraped opening bid as the current purchase price. Every county states the listed amount is stale and only the Clerk (or, in Levy/Indian River, the Tax Collector) can quote the live figure. Store the published phone/email (e.g., Volusia 386-736-5919; Palm Beach 561-355-2962; Broward RTT 954-831-4000) as a required field so the price is always re-confirmed at acquisition time.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** if a county's register grows past a few dozen rows (audit value rises), promote it up the queue; if a "static" page silently converts to a JS portal (a redesign), demote it to Stage 3 and re-verify. Re-verify every URL before each production run — Florida clerks migrate between Pioneer, RealAuction, and in-house systems without notice.

## Caveats
- **Verified vs. inferred.** I fetched and confirmed live content for Indian River (2-row table with the exact audit fields), Putnam (full parcel table), Columbia/Gulf/Hamilton/Wakulla/Nassau (empty "none on list" statements), and Bay (portal with field list). All other rows rest on official-domain search results — high confidence for the domain and the existence of a tax-deed page, but the exact register URL/format was not fetched end-to-end and is marked "Partial." Martin's and Gadsden's RealTaxDeed subdomains are pattern-inferred and unverified. Monroe and Okeechobee registers could not be confirmed at all.
- **Enricher-confirmed quotes.** Escambia's $7 quote fee, Broward's "up to three quotes per individual per day … typically available within 3-5 business days," Hillsborough's Thursday 10 a.m. weekly sales and `YYYY-CaseNumber` spreadsheet format, Charlotte's "every Tuesday starting at 9:00 a.m.," and Manatee's first-business-day-of-month price update are direct quotes from the respective official sites. Broward's Lands Available is a County **Records, Taxes & Treasury / Tax Collector** function, not a Clerk function — an important structural exception to the "Clerk maintains LAFT" rule.
- **Row counts** are reported only where visible at research time (Volusia 11, Indian River 2, Putnam ~35+); lists fluctuate and several small counties currently show zero.
- **co_no numbering** follows the Florida DOR alphabetical scheme (11 Alachua … 77 Washington; Miami-Dade = 23, historically "Dade"). These are stable and reliable.
- **DOR county-number source** (floridarevenue.com CountyNumberMap.pdf) blocks automated fetch; the numbers here were cross-checked against a DOR tax-receipts table that lists them inline (11 Alachua, 12 Baker, 13 Bay, 27 Escambia, 74 Volusia, etc.), so they are corroborated but not taken from the canonical map PDF directly.
- **Third-party lookalikes** (taxsaleresources, taxnetusa, propertychecker, floridapropertyrecords, visionarysurplusrecovery, countyoffice, registryofdeeds) were excluded throughout; only official Clerk domains and legitimate official vendors (RealAuction, LienHub, Bid4Assets, Grant Street) appear in the table.
- **Register-type completeness.** This work-list is strongest on the primary target (Lands Available) and the auction platform per county. Surplus Funds URLs were captured where directly seen (Bay, Brevard, Collier, Lee, Osceola, Pasco, Pinellas) but were not exhaustively hunted for all 67; treat blank Surplus cells as "not researched," not "not published."
